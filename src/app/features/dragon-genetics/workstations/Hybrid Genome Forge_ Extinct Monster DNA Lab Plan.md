# Hybrid Genome Forge: Extinct Monster DNA Lab

## Mission

The Dragon Genetics Laboratory has recovered **damaged DNA fragments from extinct monsters** preserved in ancient bones, frozen tissue, scales, and fossilized eggs.

Students choose one living dragon as their **base dragon**. Their goal is to identify useful genes in the extinct DNA archive and create a viable hybrid dragon for a specific mission.

Examples:

- Survive extreme cold
- Hunt in darkness
- Resist poisonous environments
- Fly at high altitude
- Survive extreme heat
- Defend the Dragon Sanctuary

The challenge is that students **cannot simply select monster traits**. They must identify the DNA responsible for the trait and determine whether adding that gene will actually produce a useful protein and phenotype.

---

# 1. Choose the Base Dragon

Students begin with one living dragon.

Each dragon has:

- Chromosome map
- Known genes
- Alleles
- Phenotypes
- Existing strengths
- Existing weaknesses
- Protein/enzyme profile
- Health conditions

Example:

### Emberwing
Strengths:
- Strong flight
- Fire resistance

Weaknesses:
- Poor night vision
- Low cold tolerance

Students receive a mission such as:

> **Create an Emberwing hybrid capable of surviving the Frozen Wastes.**

---

# 2. Extinct Monster DNA Archive

Create 6–10 fictional extinct creatures.

Examples:

### Frost Titan
Possible traits:
- antifreeze-like blood protein
- thick insulating scales
- cold-resistant metabolism

### Cave Basilisk
Possible traits:
- exceptional low-light vision
- venom resistance

### Thunder Roc
Possible traits:
- massive wings
- high-altitude breathing adaptations

### Ash Wyrm
Possible traits:
- extreme heat resistance
- armored scales

### Shadow Stalker
Possible traits:
- dark pigmentation
- enhanced night vision
- silent movement

### Deep Leviathan
Possible traits:
- oxygen storage
- pressure tolerance
- underwater respiration adaptations

Students should not immediately know which DNA fragment controls which trait.

---

# 3. Ancient DNA Scanner

Students examine fictional DNA fragments recovered from each extinct monster.

For example:

**Frost Titan Sample**

Fragment A  
`ATG...`

Fragment B  
`ATG...`

Fragment C  
`ATG...`

The interface provides clues from:

- fossil evidence
- protein traces
- comparison with living species
- chromosome location
- phenotype records

Students determine which fragment is most likely connected to the desired trait.

This should feel like a **genetics investigation**, not a menu saying “click Cold Resistance.”

---

# 4. Compare the Gene

Students place:

**Living Dragon Gene**

beside:

**Extinct Monster Gene**

They compare DNA sequences and identify similarities and differences.

This can reinforce:

- genes
- DNA sequences
- alleles
- mutations
- chromosome locations
- genotype vs. phenotype

Some extinct fragments should contain mutations or missing sections so students have to select the most promising sample.

---

# 5. Predict the Protein

Before adding any extinct DNA, students should determine what the gene probably does.

Run the selected fragment through:

**DNA → mRNA → amino acids → protein**

Students can compare it with a known working dragon gene.





For example:

### Frost Titan Gene

Predicted protein:

**Cryozyme**

Function:

Helps cells continue functioning at extremely low temperatures.

Now students know *why* the gene could help rather than simply being told it creates “cold resistance.”

---

# 6. Genome Forge

The student opens the chromosome map of their living dragon.

The extinct gene appears as a draggable **DNA fragment card**.

Students choose a compatible fictional gene location and place the extinct DNA into the hybrid genome.

Keep this conceptual rather than modeling a real laboratory gene-editing protocol.

The interface should emphasize:

> **Adding DNA changes the genetic instructions. Those instructions may change which proteins the dragon produces.**

---

# 7. Gene Compatibility Check

Not every monster gene should work perfectly in every dragon.

After adding a gene, students run:

### GENOME STABILITY TEST

Possible results:

**Compatible**
- Protein produced correctly

**Low Expression**
- Protein is produced, but not enough

**Protein Conflict**
- New protein interferes with an existing dragon function

**Nonfunctional**
- Gene produces a defective protein

**Unexpected Trait**
- Gene affects another phenotype

This creates scientific tradeoffs.

---

# 8. Limited Genetic Changes

Students should only get about **3 extinct DNA additions**.

This is important.

Otherwise students will simply build a dragon with every available superpower.

Example:

### Genetic Modification Capacity

**0 / 3 Extinct Genes Added**

Students must choose the genes that best solve their mission.

---

# 9. Hidden Gene Effects

Some genes should have secondary effects.

Example:

### Frost Titan Insulation Gene

Benefit:
+ Cold resistance

Tradeoff:
− Heat tolerance

### Thunder Roc Wing Gene

Benefit:
+ Larger wings

Tradeoff:
− Increased energy requirement

### Basilisk Night Vision Gene

Benefit:
+ Excellent dark vision

Tradeoff:
− Light sensitivity

Students therefore learn that **genes affect biological systems**, not video-game statistics.

---

# 10. Mutation Risk

Some recovered extinct DNA should contain mutations.

Students may discover:

> The Frost Titan sample contains a mutation.

They compare normal and mutated sequences to determine what happened.





Possible outcomes:

- Silent mutation
- Amino acid change
- Premature stop
- Frameshift

Students decide:

**Use this DNA or reject it?**

---

# 11. Grow the Hybrid

Once students finalize the genome:

### ACTIVATE INCUBATOR

The system creates the hybrid embryo.

Show development checkpoints such as:

**Gene expression**

→

**Protein production**

→

**Developing structures**

→

**Juvenile dragon**

→

**Adult hybrid**

Do not immediately reveal whether every modification worked.

---

# 12. Hybrid Examination

Students examine their new dragon.

Display:

### Genotype

Original dragon genes + extinct DNA additions.

### Proteins Produced

Which new proteins are functioning?

### Phenotype

What traits actually appeared?

### Unexpected Effects

What changed that the student did not anticipate?

---

# 13. Hybrid Testing Chamber

The hybrid then faces the mission.

For a Frozen Wastes mission:

### Test 1
-10°C

### Test 2
-30°C

### Test 3
Low food availability

### Test 4
Ice storm

Students collect performance data.

Their monster hybrid may perform extremely well in one test and poorly in another.

---

# 14. Failure Becomes Part of the Investigation

A hybrid that fails should not simply get:

**Wrong Answer**

Instead show evidence.

Example:

> **Body temperature dropped rapidly.**

Student investigates:

- Did the selected gene produce a protein?
- Was the protein functional?
- Was the trait actually controlled by another gene?
- Did another modification interfere?
- Did the student misinterpret the fossil evidence?

They can return to the Genome Forge and redesign.

---

# 15. Extinct Monster Mystery

Do not completely identify all extinct DNA before the lab.

For example:

### UNKNOWN SAMPLE X-17

Recovered from:
Frozen cave

Fossil evidence:
Large predator

Protein evidence:
Possible temperature-related protein

Closest matching genes:
- metabolic enzyme
- scale protein
- oxygen transport protein

Students have to determine what the DNA probably did.

This gives the workstation the **mad scientist / ancient DNA mystery** feel.

---

# 16. Final Hybrid Dossier

At the end, students create a short hybrid record:

### Hybrid Name

### Extinct Species DNA Used

### Genes Added

### Proteins Produced

### Resulting Traits

### Mutations Found

> **Why is this hybrid genetically suited for the mission?**

# Best Gameplay Loop

**Choose dragon**

→ **Receive survival mission**

→ **Search extinct monster archive**

→ **Analyze DNA fragments**

→ **Identify promising genes**

→ **Predict the proteins**

→ **Add limited extinct genes**

→ **Check for mutations**

→ **Grow hybrid**

→ **Observe phenotype**

→ **Test hybrid**

→ **Analyze failures**

→ **Modify design**

→ **Create final monster-dragon hybrid**

The important part is that students are not choosing **“night vision + cold resistance + armor.”**

They are choosing **genes**, figuring out what proteins those genes produce, and then discovering what phenotype results.

That makes the mad-scientist fantasy sit directly on top of the genetics content.
