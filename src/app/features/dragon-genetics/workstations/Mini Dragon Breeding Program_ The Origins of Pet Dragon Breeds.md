# Mini Dragon Breeding Program

## Project Premise

Students become breeders at the **Royal Mini Dragon Society**, an organization responsible for maintaining and developing healthy breeds of domesticated miniature dragons.

The project begins with the story of how humans first domesticated wild mini dragons. Over many generations, people selectively bred dragons for traits such as:

- small size
- friendly temperament
- unusual colors
- floppy or curled horns
- large eyes
- fluffy scales
- short snouts
- wing shape
- tail shape
- fire color
- low fire output
- intelligence
- playfulness

Students then inherit a small population of modern mini dragons and must use genetics to establish their **own recognizable pet dragon breed**.

The goal is not simply to make the cutest dragon. Students need to create a population in which the desired traits are **reliably inherited**.

---

# Opening Lore: The First Mini Dragons

Hundreds of years ago, tiny wild dragons lived around villages.

Unlike their enormous relatives, these dragons were about the size of cats. They survived by stealing:

- eggs
- grain
- scraps of meat
- warm places near fires

Most wild mini dragons were nervous, aggressive, brown-gray animals with long wings and sharp horns.

Occasionally, however, people noticed unusual individuals.

Some were:

- calmer around humans
- smaller
- unusually colored
- playful
- less likely to breathe dangerous flames

Villagers began keeping the friendliest dragons.

Instead of allowing every dragon to reproduce, they bred dragons with traits they liked.

Generation after generation, the dragons changed.

Eventually, completely different **breeds of domesticated mini dragons** appeared.

Now the Royal Mini Dragon Society maintains dozens of breeds, and a new generation of breeders is being trained.

That is where the students enter.

---

# Adventure 1: The Domestication Files

Students examine old illustrations and genetic records showing the transition from the **Wild Mini Dragon** to early domesticated dragons.

They compare several generations.

### Wild Population

Mostly:

- large
- gray/brown
- long horns
- strong fire
- nervous temperament

Rare variations exist:

- small body
- white scales
- curled horns
- calm behavior
- fluffy neck scales

Students identify:

> **Which variations would early humans have intentionally bred?**

### Genetics concept

**Variation + artificial selection**

Students discover that humans did not create the traits. Variation already existed, and breeders changed how common those traits became by choosing which dragons reproduced.

# Adventure 2: Meet the Foundation Dragons

Students receive a population of perhaps **12–16 mini dragons**.

Each has:

- portrait
- name
- sex
- phenotype
- pedigree
- known or partially known genotype
- health information

Example:

### Biscuit

Phenotype:
- cream scales
- curled horns
- friendly
- medium wings

Known genotype:
- `Cc`
- `hh`
- several unknown loci

### Pepper

Phenotype:
- black scales
- straight horns
- extremely small

Students investigate the genetics behind their available dragons before deciding what type of breed they want.

# Adventure 3: Design the Breed Standard

Students create their desired breed.

They choose perhaps **4 major traits**, not unlimited traits.

Example:

## Marshmallow Dragon

Desired breed standard:

- very small body
- white scales
- curled horns
- fluffy neck
- gentle temperament

Or:

## Midnight Pocket Wyvern

- black scales
- enormous eyes
- short wings
- blue fire
- playful temperament

Students now have a genetic target.

---

# Adventure 4: Trait Genetics Laboratory

Students investigate how each desired trait is inherited.

Different traits should intentionally use different inheritance patterns.

### Simple Dominance

Curled horns:

`H = curled`

`h = straight`

### Recessive

Fluffy scales:

`f = fluffy`

`F = smooth`

### Incomplete Dominance

Wing size:

`WW = large`

`Ww = medium`

`ww = tiny`

### Codominance

Scale patterns:

`B = black patches`

`G = gold patches`

`BG = black-and-gold patterned`

### Multiple Alleles

Fire color could have several alleles.

This allows the breed-development challenge to cover much more genetics than one repeated Punnett-square pattern.

---

# Adventure 5: Find Hidden Alleles

Some foundation dragons carry recessive traits that students need.

For example:

The student wants fluffy scales.

None of their dragons look fluffy.

But pedigree records suggest several descend from an old fluffy champion.

Students use the **Pedigree Lab** to locate likely carriers.

Now the breed project connects to the elusive recessive-gene investigation.

---

# Adventure 6: Breeding Planner

Students choose two dragons.

Before breeding, they must predict offspring.

Show:

### Parent 1
Genotype

### Parent 2
Genotype

Students predict:

- possible genotypes
- possible phenotypes
- probability of desired offspring

Then they authorize breeding.

The resulting offspring are generated probabilistically.

---

# Adventure 7: Meiosis Hatchery

Instead of offspring simply appearing, students can enter the Meiosis Hatchery.

They watch chromosomes separate and crossing over create different gametes.

They inspect the four possible gametes from each parent and select an egg and sperm for the breeding chamber.

This reinforces that offspring inherit **one allele for each gene from each parent**.

---

# Adventure 8: The Nursery

The egg hatches.

Students see the new baby dragon before seeing all of its genetic information.

For example:

## PIP HAS HATCHED!

Observed:

- tiny body
- cream scales
- curled horns
- smooth scales
- blue eyes

Students compare the offspring to their breed goal.

### Breed Match

3 of 5 target traits

Now they decide whether Pip should become:

- breeding stock
- pet-only
- future champion
- genetic carrier worth keeping

This makes each hatch meaningful.

---

# Adventure 9: Build the Breed Across Generations

Students cannot create a breed from one dragon.

They must breed through several simulated generations.

Example:

### Generation 1
Find useful alleles.

### Generation 2
Combine desired traits.

### Generation 3
Increase consistency.

### Generation 4
Establish the breed.

A timeline could visually populate with the student's dragons as the breed develops.

---

# Adventure 10: The Genetic Diversity Problem

Students eventually discover a major problem.

The dragon with the most desirable traits might be closely related to all their other good dragons.

Breeding only the cutest dragons begins increasing harmful recessive disorders.

Give the program a:

## Genetic Diversity Meter

High → Healthy

Moderate → Warning

Low → Inbreeding Risk

Students now have to balance:

**appearance + desired genes + health + diversity**

This prevents the breeding activity from becoming pure trait optimization.

---

# Adventure 11: Unexpected Genetic Disorders

Some recessive alleles should produce health conditions.

Example:

`d = digestive enzyme deficiency`

A dragon can be:

`DD` healthy  
`Dd` healthy carrier  
`dd` affected

Suddenly two beautiful champion dragons may be a poor match because both carry `d`.

Students can investigate affected offspring through the **Protein Synthesis / Diet Rescue Lab**.

That ties another workstation directly into the breeding program.

---

# Adventure 12: Mutation Surprise

Occasionally an offspring receives a new mutation.

Example:

### NEW MUTATION DETECTED

Pip has unusual star-shaped scale markings never recorded in this bloodline.

Students investigate whether the mutation:

- changes phenotype
- does nothing noticeable
- creates a health problem
- could become the basis of a new breed trait

This introduces mutations naturally instead of teaching them separately.

---

# Final Challenge: Mini Dragon Show

Students enter their new breeds in a virtual dragon show.

Categories could include:

### Most Adorable

Student voting.

### Best Breed Consistency

How closely offspring match the standard.

### Healthiest Bloodline

Genetic diversity + low disorder risk.

### Rarest Trait

Genetics-based award.

### Best Genetic Strategy

Quality of student reasoning.

### Best New Breed

Combination of all categories.

Winning should not determine the grade. The student's genetics evidence should.

# Great Opportunity for Different Breed Types

The program could eventually contain established breeds students encounter before making their own.

### Cloud Puff
Tiny, fluffy white dragon with very weak fire.

### Ember Pug
Stocky dragon with short snout and enormous personality.

### Shadow Sprite
Small black dragon with huge eyes and excellent night vision.

### Highland Floof
Large fluffy miniature dragon adapted to cold climates.

### Ruby Crest
Red dragon bred for dramatic horns and bright scale coloration.

### Pocket Wyvern
Very small, athletic dragon with oversized wings.

### Golden Snugglehorn
Calm temperament, curled horns, and gold scales.

### Speckled Moonwing
Spotted scales and large silver wings.

These breeds could have recognizable genetic histories, including founders, recessive traits, and known disorders.

# Best Overall Structure

The **Pet Dragon Breeding Program becomes the main shell around several genetics workstations**:

**Domestication Archive**
→ variation & artificial selection

**Trait Lab**
→ alleles, dominance and inheritance patterns

**Pedigree Archive**
→ recessive alleles and carriers

**Meiosis Hatchery**
→ segregation, crossing over and gametes

**Breeding Chamber**
→ Punnett squares and probability

**Nursery**
→ genotype vs. phenotype

**DNA Analyzer**
→ mutations

**Dragon Clinic**
→ genes → proteins → health

**Breed Registry**
→ genetic diversity and artificial selection

That could make the pet-dragon program especially strong because students spend the entire genetics unit working toward one simple goal:

> **Start with ordinary mini dragons and use genetics to establish the most adorable new dragon breed possible without creating an unhealthy bloodline.**
